Someone to learn things with.

Early — the skeleton is built, the product is not

Step 1 of 5

Accessibility is the way in, not a later pass.

Saathi (साथी) means companion. The premise is that the qualities which make software usable for someone who needs them — patience, saying what is happening out loud, never demanding a precise click, working entirely by voice — are the same qualities that make a good companion for anyone learning something new.

So the accessibility framing is not a compliance pass bolted on at the end. It is the thing that makes the product good at all.

Step 2 of 5

It always says where you are.

Someone who cannot see a progress bar still needs to know how much is left. Someone who can see one is not harmed by hearing it. So every step announces its own position, every time:

An example exchange.

You

Teach me to make dal.

Saathi

Step 1 of 4. Rinse the lentils until the water runs clear. Takes about a minute.

You

Slower.

Saathi

Of course. Still step 1 of 4. Put them in a bowl, cover with cold water, and swirl with your hand.

  • It narrates

    Saying what is happening is the primary output, not an accessibility affordance layered over a visual one.

  • It can be interrupted

    “Slower”, “again”, “skip that” are first-class. A companion you cannot interrupt is a lecture.

  • It cannot be misheard into harm

    Every choice a mishearing could land on is a closed set. A wrong word gives a wrong value, never a wrong command.

  • It refuses out loud

    When Saathi will not do something, it says so and says why, rather than failing quietly.

Step 3 of 5

Mac and Windows, from one agreement.

Saathi has two native clients. They share no code — one is Swift, one is C# — so the only thing holding them together is a written contract, and nothing about a contract is checked by a compiler.

So it is generated. One schema produces the Swift, the C# and the TypeScript, and the build fails if what is committed no longer matches. A script runs both clients side by side and compares what they say. Keeping the two honest is a build step, not a habit.

Step 4 of 5

What actually exists today.

Honestly: the frame, and not the thing yet. This page would rather say so than imply a product that is not there.

  • The generated contract, and the check that keeps it honest Built
  • Backend holding the provider keys Built
  • macOS and Windows clients, agreeing command for command Built
  • The companion itself — voice, listening, actually teaching anything Not started
Step 5 of 5

Four questions, still open.

These are genuinely undecided, and they are the reason there is no product yet. If you have a view — especially a first-hand one — it is worth more right now than any amount of code.

  1. Who is it for first? Accessibility covers vision, motor, cognitive, hearing and situational needs, and they pull the design in different directions. Picking one to start is what makes the first version good at anything.

  2. What does “learn and play” mean concretely? Learning an app, a skill, a subject, a device? Playing as exploration without consequence, or as games?

  3. Where does it live? A desktop app, a phone, the web, a device. This follows from the first two and should not be picked before them.

  4. What is the smallest thing that would already be useful to one real person?

Patience is a feature. So is saying where you are.
